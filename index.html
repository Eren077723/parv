🌌 What is Dimensional Analysis?

📖 Definition

Dimensional Analysis is a mathematical technique used in physics to study the relationship between different physical quantities by identifying their fundamental dimensions (Mass, Length, Time, etc.) and units. It helps us check the correctness of equations, derive relationships between physical quantities, and convert units from one system to another — without even performing an experiment!


Every physical quantity can be expressed in terms of seven base dimensions: Mass [M], Length [L], Time [T], Electric Current [A], Temperature [K], Amount of Substance [mol], and Luminous Intensity [cd]. In Class 11, we mainly work with M, L, and T.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Fundamental (Base) Quantities: Mass, Length, Time, Temperature, Current, Luminous Intensity, Amount of Substance.
  • Derived Quantities: Quantities derived from base quantities, e.g., Speed = Length/Time.
  • Dimensional Formula: An expression showing how a physical quantity depends on base quantities, e.g., Force = [M¹L¹T⁻²].
  • Dimensional Equation: An equation obtained by equating a physical quantity with its dimensional formula.

🎯 Principle of Homogeneity

This principle states that only quantities with the same dimensions can be added, subtracted, or equated. In any correct physical equation, the dimensions on the Left-Hand Side (LHS) must equal the dimensions on the Right-Hand Side (RHS).

[LHS] = [RHS]   (Dimensionally)

Example: In s = ut + ½at², every term (s, ut, ½at²) must have dimension [L].

🎬 Visual Example: Simple Pendulum

Deriving Time Period Using Dimensional Analysis

Let's assume the time period T of a simple pendulum depends on:

  • Mass of bob (m)
  • Length of string (l)
  • Acceleration due to gravity (g)

We assume: T = k · ma · lb · gc

Step 1: Write dimensions of each quantity:
[T] = [M⁰L⁰T¹], [m] = [M¹L⁰T⁰], [l] = [M⁰L¹T⁰], [g] = [M⁰L¹T⁻²]
Step 2: Substituting into the equation:
[M⁰L⁰T¹] = [M¹]a [L¹]b [L¹T⁻²]c
Step 3: Compare powers of M, L, T:
M: a = 0   |   L: b + c = 0   |   T: -2c = 1 → c = -1/2
Step 4: Solve: b = -c = 1/2, so b = 1/2
T = k √(l/g)

(Experimentally, k = 2π, giving the famous formula T = 2π√(l/g))

🚀 Applications of Dimensional Analysis

✅

Checking Correctness

Verify whether a physical equation is dimensionally correct by comparing dimensions on both sides.

🔄

Unit Conversion

Convert units from one system (CGS/FPS) to another (SI) accurately using dimensional formulas.

🧮

Deriving Relations

Derive formulas connecting different physical quantities, like T = 2π√(l/g) for a pendulum.

🔬

Finding Dimensions of Constants

Determine the dimensional formula of unknown physical constants (like Gravitational constant G).

🌡️

Engineering Design

Used in designing models and scaling in engineering (aircraft, ships) via dimensionless numbers.

🛰️

Astrophysics & Space Science

Helps estimate quantities in astrophysics where direct measurement is impossible, like stellar masses.

⚠️ Limitations of Dimensional Analysis

1

It does not give information about the dimensionless constants (like 2π, ½) in the formula.

2

It cannot be used if a physical quantity depends on more than 3 factors (since we only have 3 equations from M, L, T).

3

It fails when the relationship involves trigonometric, exponential, or logarithmic functions.

4

It cannot distinguish between physical quantities having the same dimensions (e.g., Work and Torque both have [ML²T⁻²]).

✏️ Solved Example

Q: Check the dimensional correctness of v² = u² + 2as

[v²] = [M⁰L¹T⁻¹]² = [M⁰L²T⁻²]
[u²] = [M⁰L¹T⁻¹]² = [M⁰L²T⁻²]
[2as] = [M⁰L¹T⁻²][M⁰L¹T⁰] = [M⁰L²T⁻²]
✅ LHS = RHS → Equation is Dimensionally Correct!
